Job Market Paper

Dams and Rural Labor Markets in India

Large dams deliver irrigation and power across wide regions, while their reservoirs submerge land in the villages that host them. This paper examines how the labor markets of these host villages adjust to reservoir formation in India. I date the first filling of 395 reservoirs from satellite records and link them to 41,507 villages observed in three population censuses. In the decade a reservoir forms, the agricultural laborer share of main workers rises by 2.9 percentage points more within 2 kilometers of the shoreline than in villages 10 to 20 kilometers from the same reservoir, a difference absent in the previous decade. The rise, about 12 percent of the initial share, is concentrated in villages that lost part of their own land. In these villages the share increases by 4.4 percentage points, cultivated land falls by about 8 percentage points of village area, and population grows about 5 percent less. Edge villages that lost no land show no detectable change. Non-farm employment and irrigated area grow no faster near the reservoir, so the composition of work shifts toward farm wage labor, the lowest-paid form of rural wage work, rather than out of farming. The results suggest that host communities bear labor market costs even as dams generate broader regional benefits.

Working Paper

Balancing Motherhood and Employment: Analyzing the Consequences of Extended Maternity Leave in India

In 2017, India extended paid maternity leave from 12 to 26 weeks under the Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act. The reform was meant to strengthen women’s attachment to paid work, but by raising the cost of employing women of childbearing age it may also have reduced their employment. Using three rounds of the National Family Health Survey (2005–2021) and a difference-in-differences design, I estimate the effect of the extension on women’s labor force participation. Participation among women of prime childbearing age fell by about 2.6 percentage points after the reform. The decline is largest among younger and more educated women, the groups most likely to hold the formal-sector jobs to which the law applies. The results suggest that employer-financed mandates can narrow the very employment they are designed to protect.

Work in Progress

What Binds Women’s Decision-Making?

An evidence review of gender-transformative interventions in developing countries, written during my research internship in the Economic Research and Development Impact Department of the Asian Development Bank. The review screens 1,130 records down to 89 studies and codes each intervention by how it acts on women’s decision-making. Presented in the ADB Economics Brownbag Seminar Series, Manila, August 2026.
