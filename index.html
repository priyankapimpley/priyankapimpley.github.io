I study how labor markets in India adjust to large policy changes such as infrastructure projects and employment mandates, combining census and survey records with satellite and spatial data. Job Market Paper

Dams and Rural Labor Markets in India

Large dams deliver irrigation and power across wide regions, while their reservoirs submerge land in the villages that host them. This paper examines how the labor markets of these host villages adjust to reservoir formation in India. In villages at the reservoir edge that lose land, work shifts within agriculture toward laboring for wages, the lowest-paid form of rural wage work, while non-farm employment and irrigated area grow no faster than in villages farther away. The costs of a dam therefore reach beyond displaced households to the villages that remain beside the reservoir. These costs are concentrated within a few kilometers of the shoreline, which matters for how dam projects are appraised and how host communities are supported.
